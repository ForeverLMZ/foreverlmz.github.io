About

Mingze Li

publishing as Leukos

I'm a PhD student in the Cognition & Perception program at New York University, advised by Grace Lindsay. My research asks how attention and curiosity shape learning — building reinforcement learning models where feature-based attention is guided by reward prediction error, and designing behavioral tasks to study how attention drives exploration and hypothesis formation in novel environments. Before NYU, I worked with Blake Richards at Mila and McGill University on connectome-informed artificial neural networks.

Research

Research Projects

Attention's Effect on Intrinsically Motivated Exploration in Human and Reinforcement Learning

New York University · May 2026 – Present

Designing a novel behavioral task (PsychoPy, with eye-tracking) to measure how feature-based attention drives human exploration and hypothesis formation in new environments. Formalizing attention as a context-dependent observation-to-state encoder for RL agents' world models, built on an adapted theory-based RL codebase (EMPA).

Reward-Prediction-Error-Guided Feature Attention

New York University · Sep. 2024 – Present · manuscript in revision at Journal of Neuroscience

A reinforcement learning model in which feature-based attention is dynamically modulated by reward prediction error, fit to primate neural and behavioral data in visual-attention tasks to explain how attention shapes decision-making and value learning.

3D Unity-Integrated Platform for Reinforcement Learning and VR Training of Humans and Animals

SilicoLabs, Toronto · Sep. 2023 – Jan. 2024 · advised by Blake Richards

Built and trained reinforcement learning models in a Unity-based 3D task environment; led the redesign of a cross-platform RL framework for VR-enabled training across human, animal, and AI models in simulated real-world environments.

Connectome-to-Model Toolbox: Bridging Brain Architecture & Artificial Networks

Mila – Quebec AI Institute · Sep. 2022 – May 2024 · published in Nature Communications (2024)

Co-developed conn2res, an open-source Python toolbox that transforms brain connectivity data into brain-inspired artificial neural network architectures.

Earlier Research

  • Animal Connectome Informed Reservoir Computing of Multiple Tasks honours thesis

    Montreal Neurological Institute · Advisor: Bratislav Misic · Sep. 2021 – Aug. 2023

    Built connectome-informed reservoir networks on five animal connectomes (C. elegans, Drosophila, mouse, marmoset, macaque) and trained them on 15 cognitive tasks from NeuroGym and Gym — the origin of the conn2res toolbox above.

  • Comparative Analysis of Sulcal Patterns in the Lateral Occipital Cortex across Humans and Chimpanzees

    Montreal Neurological Institute · Advisors: Michael Petrides & Kep Kee Loh · May 2022 – Dec. 2023

    Labeled vision-related sulci in fMRI data across primate species using FreeSurfer and wrote Python analysis scripts to compare sulcal patterns across species.

  • Macapype: Python-Based Pipelines for Multi-Modal Brain MRI Anatomical Data Analysis

    Institut des Neurosciences de la Timone, Marseille (remote) · Advisors: David Meunier & Kep Kee Loh · Sep. 2022 – Apr. 2023

    Quality-checked marmoset fMRI segmentations generated by the Macapype pipeline and corrected pipeline-generated T1 gray/white matter output against the source images.

  • Decision Making with Brain-Inspired Recurrent Neural Networks

    IDG/McGovern Institute at Peking University · Advisor: Zhou Yang · May – Sep. 2021

    Co-programmed a bio-inspired RNN modeling decision-making in areas LIP and FEF, and independently designed and ran human psychophysical decision-making experiments with eye tracking (Psychtoolbox).
