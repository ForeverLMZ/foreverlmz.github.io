About

Cognition & Perception, NYU

I'm a PhD student in the Cognition & Perception program at NYU's Center for Neural Science, advised by Grace W. Lindsay. My work sits at the intersection of reinforcement learning, attention, and curiosity: how does an agent — biological or artificial — decide what's worth looking at, and how does that choice shape what it learns next? I'm supported by the Henry M. MacCracken Fellowship and the TPCN (Theoretical Principles in Cognition and Neuroscience) Fellowship.

Research

Current lines

Attention · Exploration

Curiosity-Driven Exploration & Attention in Reinforcement Learning

May 2026 — present

Formalizing attention as a context-dependent observation-to-state encoder, and studying how it drives hypothesis generation and world-model learning under uncertainty. Built the Color Cluster Escape Task (PsychoPy, with integrated eye-tracking) and Quantum Rainbow, an RL gridworld for multidimensional curiosity-driven exploration; deployed a theory-based RL codebase (EMPA) on NYU's Greene HPC cluster. Ongoing collaboration with Marcelo Mattar and Mark Ho.

Attention · Value Learning

Reward-Prediction-Error-Guided Feature Attention

Sep 2024 — present

A reinforcement-learning model in which feature-based attention is dynamically modulated by reward prediction error, fit to primate behavioral data to explain selective-attention learning curves. Currently in revision at Journal of Neuroscience.
